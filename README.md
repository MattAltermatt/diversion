# 🌀 Diversion

A personal web gallery of small, screensaver-like generative-art pieces — **diversions**. Each one is unrelated in content (a particle flow field today, a cellular automaton tomorrow), but they all share one framework and one design ethos. The framework owns the *chrome*; a diversion only has to draw.

## What's here

- **A framework** that gives every diversion two screens for free:
  - **Config screen** — controls beside a live preview on a wide screen; on a narrow one the preview pins to the top and the controls flow beneath it.
  - **Animation screen** — a full-viewport canvas (fullscreen-able) whose entire configuration lives in the URL, so any look is a shareable link.
- **Usable on a phone, and installable** — the gallery, config and animation screens all reflow for a small touch screen, a **Keep the screen awake** toggle stops a propped-up phone going dark mid-piece, and a web app manifest means "Add to Home Screen" runs a diversion chromeless, with no browser bar. (On iPhone that is the *only* chromeless route, since Safari implements fullscreen on iPad only.)
- **One hundred thirty-seven diversions:**
  - **Ablation** (`kind: '2d'`) — a quantized contour-map picture sits in the middle; a rectangular track just outside it carries a permanent **fleet of turrets that circulate rather than expire**, each hunting one palette colour. A turret may only ever strike the **outermost surviving cell** of the lane it is passing, and only once before it must move on — so it cannot drill, erosion is a receding surface, and the picture comes apart in *layers*: the outer band has to be peeled before anything tuned to the band beneath it has a target. The whole fleet is crewed once per picture, at picture start, split across colours in proportion to the **whole map** — 50% blue means half the crew is blue — and every band is guaranteed at least one turret, so a rounding sliver can never starve a colour and hang the piece forever — a guarantee Mixed needs and Unison does not, since a Unison crew is one colour by construction and moves on, which is why asking Unison for two turrets gets you exactly two while Mixed quietly puts the shortfall in the queue. A turret keeps its colour for the picture's life; once that colour's cells are gone everywhere it **retires** rather than riding out blank shifts hunting nothing. One tempering exponent (**Targeting bias**) shapes that initial split — flat, proportional, or piled onto the dominant colour — and doubles as the weighting for **Unison** mode's lock draw (below). A turret's brightness on the track is the charge it has left; on running dry, riding a whole lap without landing a single strike, or hitting the lap cap, it rotates out at the gate — never destroyed, just reset to full charge and sent to the **back of the queue** for another shift. The crew size is set by the two counts you can read straight off the screen — **Turrets on track** and **In queue** — and the total is simply their sum, so the queue is never a subtraction you have to do in your head; the waiting turrets are drawn as a trail of dots peeling back from the top-left corner; a trail of smaller dots from the diagonally opposite corner is the retired row, which visibly fills as the map empties and clears again at the next picture (the two rows differ by size rather than brightness — dimming pushed the darker half of every ramp under the contrast floor the palettes are built to hold). **Targeting** switches between **Mixed** (the fixed proportional crew above, working every band at once) and **Unison** (the whole crew locked onto one exposed colour at a time, chosen by the same tempered weighted draw and released only once that colour leaves the surface — a switch shows up first as a new colour entering the back of the queue, before it ever reaches the track, which is the mode's whole visible tell). Misses are silent and free, so a strike reads as an event. The track's offset from the picture produces **corner dead zones** with no corner logic — the ends of each edge sit past the picture, so their beams find nothing. When the last cell goes there is nothing left to shoot, so the track drains and turns quiet for a beat before the next picture floods in and the fleet re-crews — an emergent rhythm with no timer behind it. Palette *length* is the band count, from two (a stark black-and-white map) to twenty-four (a slow topographic dissolve), and every ramp deliberately stops short of the ground — quantization is by quantile, so a near-black darkest stop would leave a *sixth* of every map indistinguishable from destroyed space. **Source** swaps that generated map for one of two picture sources. **Pictures** works through a curated set of CC0 **pixel-art sprites** bundled with the gallery — a sword, a potion, a crown, a little villager — each blown up so one sprite pixel is a block of cells, matted into a solid rectangle the turrets have to eat through, and moving on to the next sprite each time one is finished. Because a 16x16 sprite has no detail left to reveal, turning **Cell size** down does not sharpen the picture at all — it just divides it into more, smaller cells, so the knob becomes purely how long the demolition takes. Unlike your own upload, this choice **does** travel in a shared link. **Yours** peels a picture of your own: pick an image and it is grouped into **Colors** bands by perceptual (OKLab) clustering — like with like — and stretched across the full dark-to-light span, so two colours really does give you black and white. An imported picture re-peels *itself* each cycle rather than being replaced, and comes apart differently every time because the crew is shuffled fresh; it also stays on your machine, since it is far too big to ride in a link — your own reloads keep it, while someone opening a link you share gets the generated contour map instead (or their own picture, if they have one on that machine). Its colours are floored well clear of the ground, so an intact band never reads as space a turret has already cleared. **Spacing** governs the formation without ever moving where a turret enters: every one joins at the top-left gate, and the knob sets how far the previous one must travel before the gate reopens — the default of 1 holds it for `perimeter / turrets-on-track` so the crew arrives already evenly spread and stays that way as it circulates; 0 releases them as one bunched pack instead. Two preset axes — **Palette** (Bathymetric / Ember / Monochrome / Verdigris / Ultraviolet / Mariners — the old-school 1977–86 royal-blue-and-gold the gallery shares with Flow Field, Squiral and Particle Life) and **Demolition** (Patient / Steady / Sentinels / Ring / Swarm / Relentless / Strip Mine).

  - **Platonic Folding** (`kind: '2d'`) — a Platonic solid's flat 2-D net folds itself shut along its hinge lines into the 3-D solid, tumbles slowly in a fixed key light so faces catch highlights as they turn, holds, then unfolds flat again — looping tetrahedron → cube → octahedron → dodecahedron → icosahedron (or one pinned solid).   - **Boids** (`kind: '2d'`) — Craig Reynolds' classic flocking: each boid steers by three local rules — **separation** (avoid crowding), **alignment** (match neighbours' heading), **cohesion** (drift toward the local centre) — and living murmurations emerge, splitting and merging across the sky.   - **Vicsek Flock** (`kind: '2d'`) — the minimal model of collective motion (Vicsek 1995): self-propelled particles at constant speed, each step aligning its heading to the **average heading of its neighbours plus angular noise η**.   - **Apollonian Gasket** (`kind: '2d'`) — mutually-tangent circles packed into every curvilinear gap by the **Descartes Circle Theorem** `(k₁+k₂+k₃+k₄)² = 2(k₁²+k₂²+k₃²+k₄²)`, recursing down to a self-similar fractal foam.   - **Lightning** (`kind: '2d'`) — branching bolts grown by the **dielectric breakdown model** (Niemeyer–Pietronero–Wiesmann 1984), DLA's physics cousin but reading as *lightning* rather than coral.   - **Starfield** (`kind: '2d'`) — the classic 3-D star-warp: stars fly toward the camera (z shrinks each frame), perspective-projected so they sweep outward from a central vanishing point, growing and brightening, respawning far away when they pass. At low warp a calm drift of dots; at high warp long hyperspace streaks. Warp-speed / star-count / spread / roll / colour (white / blue-white / spectrum) knobs (#31).
  - **Hypercube** (`kind: '2d'`) — a rotating **tesseract** (4-cube: 16 vertices, 32 edges) turning through the 4th dimension — rotations in the xw/yw/zw planes make it swell inside-out, the small inner cube becoming the outer cube and back — projected 4-D→3-D→2-D by two perspective divides and drawn as glowing depth-shaded wireframe. Also renders the 16-cell and penteract; rotation-speed / projection-distance / zoom knobs (#104).
  - **Force-Directed Graph** (`kind: '2d'`) — a tangle relaxes into structure: a random graph (tree / clustered / small-world) is laid out by a Fruchterman–Reingold spring-electrical force model — every node repels, every edge springs to a rest length — so from a scramble the network untangles, communities bloom into separated clusters, and it settles into an elegant framed diagram before reseeding. Node-count / repulsion / spring / community-colour knobs (#186).
  - **Tour** (`kind: '2d'`) — an evolving Travelling-Salesman loop: cities scattered on the plane, a random closed tour continuously improved by **2-opt** / Or-opt local moves (uncross crossing edges), so a chaotic self-crossing scribble visibly untangles into an elegant crossing-free loop — then holds and rescatters. Rainbow-by-position path with edge-rewire flashes; city-count / layout / optimizer / speed knobs (#181).
  - **Lyapunov** (`kind: 'webgl'`) — the **Markus–Lyapunov** "Zircon Zity" fractal: for each (a,b) pixel the logistic map `x←r·x·(1−x)` is iterated with r alternating between a and b per a fixed binary **sequence**, and the Lyapunov exponent λ colours the plane — stable λ<0 into warm glowing organic ridges, chaotic λ>0 into deep voids — the window slowly drifting through the filigree band with a shimmering palette. Sequence (7 signatures) / zoom / iterations / palette knobs (#52).
  - **Pinion** (`kind: '2d'`, xscreensaver `pinion`) — a clockwork of interlocking gears: a connected train where each gear meshes with its neighbour (centre distance = sum of pitch radii, tooth counts set the gear ratio, phase aligned by the sum-invariant so teeth drop cleanly into gaps) turning in perfect mesh in alternating directions, forever. Brass / copper / jewel / steel styles; gear-count / tooth-range / layout (chain / cluster / ring) / drive-speed knobs (#113).
  - **Paper Marbling** (`kind: 'webgl'`) — Ebru & Suminagashi by exact closed-form maths (Aubrey Jaffer): ink drops bloom (each pushes the whole plane radially out), then whirlpools swirl, rakes shear, or a stylus drags the field — every pixel is coloured by walking the drop/comb/vortex map stack **backward** to its pre-image, so it's resolution-perfect and fully seed-deterministic (no fluid sim). Sheet builds, holds, dissolves, remarbles. Nine patterns — concentric rainbow rings · drift (drops + periodic whirlpool/rake) · whirlpool · stylus · nonpareil · feather · bouquet · stone · get-gel — up to 220 drops & 32 inks; drop / rake / swirl / sharpness knobs + Turkish-Ebru / Suminagashi / Venetian / full-spectrum / bold / nightfall palettes (#249).
  - **Hyperbolic Tiling** (`kind: '2d'`) — an Escher-style **Circle Limit** tessellation of the hyperbolic plane: a regular **{p,q}** polygon (p sides, q meeting at each vertex) sits at the centre and the whole tiling is grown by its **reflection group** — repeatedly inverting tiles across the circular arcs (geodesics orthogonal to the boundary) that form each edge, breadth-first and deduplicated, out to a capped ring count. A slow **Möbius transformation** (a hyperbolic translation whose axis gently swirls) carries every tile from the centre out toward the ever-more-crowded boundary, forever — the classic infinite Circle-Limit drift, never a static picture. Tiles colour by ring-distance-from-centre ramp with alternating reflection-parity darkening for the woodcut checkerboard read. Fill / outline / both render styles. Two preset axes — **Tiling** ({7,3} / {5,4} / {4,5} / {6,4} / {8,3} / {3,7}) and **Palette** (Escher Ink / Circle Limit Fire / Deep Sea / Aurora / Mono) (#250).
  - **Contagion** (`kind: '2d'`) — an **SIR / SIRS epidemic** on a toroidal lattice. Each cell is **S**usceptible, **I**nfected, or **R**ecovered: per step an infected cell infects each susceptible neighbour with probability **β**, stays infectious for a fixed period, recovers, and — with waning immunity (SIRS) — becomes susceptible again after an immunity period, or stays immune forever (SIR, immunity = 0). Past the transmission threshold a few sparks become a travelling wavefront; waning immunity curls the fronts into **endless spiral reinfection waves**, while permanent immunity lets each epidemic **burn out** before a fresh one seeds — so it never stalls. A single continuous phase ramp (resting field → recovering tail → bright front) plus phosphor smoothing renders the discrete automaton as a glowing liquid wave, and a live **S/I/R stacked bar + infected-fraction history** breathes below (the epidemic curve). Moore/Von-Neumann neighbourhoods; β / infectious-period / immunity-period / initial-foci knobs. Two preset axes — **Regime** (Spiral Fever / Endemic Churn / Burnout (SIR) / Fragile Spark) and **Palette** (Bloom / Toxic / Cryo / Ferroin) (#188).
  - **Swirling Magic** (`kind: '2d'`) — an homage to the **After Dark** "Rose" / "Satori" swirl screensavers (Berkeley Systems, 1989+; clean-room, credited). Luminous kaleidoscopic ribbons braid and unwind around a slowly-turning centre while colour oozes through a cyclic palette and ghost-trails smear behind, over a dark ground. One shared pipeline — **fade-trail persistence buffer + N-fold dihedral kaleidoscope + core+halo additive glow + cyclic OKLCH-ish palette LUT** — feeds **two generators**: **Vortex** (ribbons advected through a shearing whirl, their trails braiding as neighbours counter-rotate — the gallery-grade flowing look) and **Web** (a morphing harmonograph line-web mirrored into a star mandala — the faithful After Dark *Rose* reconstruction). Three incommensurate periods (spin / breathe / hue-drift) keep it from ever visibly repeating. Symmetry / mirror / swirl-shear / trail-fade / glow / colour-cycle knobs; two preset axes — **Style** (Vortex Ribbons / Tight Whirl / Faithful Web / Dense Mandala) and **Palette** (Spectrum / Aurora / Ember / Ultraviolet) (#130).
  - **Thornbird** (`kind: '2d'`, xscreensaver `thornbird`) — Clifford Pickover's "Bird in a Thornbush" iterated map (Tim Auckland's xscreensaver hack). A single scalar sequence recurs three steps deep — `x[n+1] = (1−c)·cos(π·a·x[n−1]) + c·x[n−2]` — and consecutive iterates are plotted as (xₙ, xₙ₊₁), the classic delay-embedding scatter that reveals the filamentary bird shape. Provably bounded (a clamp on the feedback coefficient **c** keeps the orbit inside [−1, 1] forever), so it never needs a validity gate. The two free coefficients — branch density **A** and feedback **C** — slowly wobble on two seeded, incommensurate periods so the bird shape continuously breathes without ever repeating. Same low-alpha additive density-plot render as the gallery's other strange-attractor pieces. Two preset axes — **Shape** (Classic Bird / Tight Thicket / Loose Wisp) and **Palette** (Frost / Ember / Mono) (#60).
  - **Chime** (`kind: '2d'`) — a still field of **wells**, each filling with energy at its own rate up to its own capacity. A full well **rings out**: it releases everything it holds as one expanding wavefront whose maximum radius *is* the energy released — so a big slow well washes the whole screen and a small one barely clears its neighbours. When that front sweeps over another well that is charged enough and out of its **refractory** period, that one releases too, dumping only what it happens to hold at that instant — so cascades produce a whole spectrum of ripple sizes, and the giants only ever happen where the field has been quiet long enough for someone to fill right up. An excitable medium whose coupling is a *travelling* front rather than an instant neighbourhood nudge; the propagation delay is what makes the patterns (chains, silences, standing interference). The wake is drawn analytically rather than as a persistence trail — an annulus filled with a radial gradient that runs from transparent at its inner edge to bright at the leading edge, plus a hairline stroke on the front itself. A decaying fade buffer looks like the obvious way to trail a moving mark, but it stalls at 8-bit rounding (a mark stops losing alpha once the per-frame decrement rounds below 1/255) and so accumulates a permanent haze over an unattended run. A single **Tempo** knob dilates time for the whole piece — charging, wave travel, refractory recovery and the wake all scale together, so the number of fronts on screen (and thus the composition at any instant) is unchanged and only the pace moves; it ships at a deep crawl (0.25), where a full-reach wavefront takes roughly three quarters of a minute to cross. Three colour sources (released energy / well identity / front age), three layouts (blue-noise / scatter / jittered lattice), additive or layered blending. Two preset axes — **Rhythm** (Zen / Cascade / Rare Giants / Chatter / Standing Waves / Tide) and **Palette** (Deep Water / Ember / Aurora / Sonar / Bruise / Ash / Paper).

## Adding a diversion

Create `src/diversions/<slug>/` with an `index.ts` that default-exports a `Diversion`. It's auto-discovered by the registry (Vite glob) — no registration step.

```ts
import type { Diversion } from '../../framework/types'
import { z } from 'zod'

const schema = z.object({
  size: z.number().int().min(1).max(100).default(20)
    .meta({ ui: 'slider', min: 1, max: 100, step: 1, label: 'Size' }),
  // ...more fields. Each field's .meta({ ui, label, help, min, max, step, options })
  // drives BOTH the form control and the URL codec.
})

const myDiversion: Diversion<typeof schema._type> = {
  id: 'my-diversion',
  title: 'My Diversion',
  description: 'One-line gallery blurb.',
  kind: '2d',                                   // '2d' | 'webgl' | 'webgpu'
  schema,
  setup(ctx, config, size) { /* build state */ return state },
  frame(state, ctx, t, dt) { /* draw ONE frame; framework owns the loop */ },
  resize(state, size, ctx) { /* optional; ctx lets you repaint on reflow */ },
  update(state, config, size) { /* optional: apply config live, return true; */
                               /* falsy → framework re-runs setup */ return true },
  teardown(state) { /* optional */ },
}
export default myDiversion
```

**The framework owns the rAF loop** and gives you pause, tab-hidden auto-pause, offscreen-tile auto-pause, `prefers-reduced-motion` politeness (static first frame + opt-in), crisp `ResizeObserver` refit, an FPS readout, and fullscreen. You just draw a frame.

### The five UX invariants (non-negotiable)

1. **Readability is key** — legible type, no decorative-over-legible tradeoffs.
2. **Discoverable, not buried** — every param stays discoverable and carries help when its effect isn't obvious. Values needn't be on-screen at all times: collapsible sections, subpanels, and `showWhen` mode-swaps (e.g. the Color panel shows palette *or* gradient controls) are all fine, as long as the control is easy to find and its value returns when you open the section / switch the controlling field.
3. **Add help when confusing** — `.meta({ help })` renders as persistent inline subtext.
4. **Sliders only when bounded** — `ui:'slider'` needs `min`/`max`; open-ended numbers use `ui:'number'`.
5. **Err toward more contrast** — high-contrast palette, crisp borders.

## Control vocabulary

`slider` · `number` · `segmented` (enum) · `select` (enum, dropdown — for long option lists) · `toggle` (bool) · `color` (swatch) · `colorList` (add/remove swatches, each with alpha) · `group` (nested) · `matrix` (config-aware grid; Particle Life's interaction table) · `hidden` (round-trips through the URL but renders no control — for preset-driven values). More get added when a diversion needs them.

Any field can carry `.meta({ showWhen: { field, equals } })` to render only when a sibling field holds a given value — used by Flow Field's Color panel to swap palette vs gradient controls by mode.

A diversion can also declare `presets?: PresetGroup[]` — named groups (independent axes) whose options each patch a subset of config. The config panel renders a dropdown per group above the form; picking one applies the patch through the normal config→URL path, and the dropdown shows "Custom" once a manual edit drifts off the preset. Flow Field ships **Flow** (motion) and **Color** (palette) groups.

_Doyle Spiral_ is built on **Robin Houston's** Doyle-spiral numerics ([gist 6096562](https://gist.github.com/robinhouston/6096562), 2013) — a clean-room TypeScript reimplementation of the Newton solve for the generators; the loxodromic-flow presentation and rendering are original to this gallery. (Doyle spirals are due to **Peter Doyle**.)

_Ablation_'s **Pictures** mode bundles pixel-art sprites released under **CC0 1.0** on [OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/): 18 items from **jetrel**'s [16x16 RPG items](https://opengameart.org/content/16x16-rpg-items) and 8 villagers from **fleurman**'s [Tiny characters set](https://opengameart.org/content/tiny-characters-set). _Neural CA_ runs the pretrained **Self-Organising Textures** neural cellular automata of **Alexander Mordvintsev & Eyvind Niklasson** ([Distill, 2021](https://distill.pub/selforg/2021/textures/)).
