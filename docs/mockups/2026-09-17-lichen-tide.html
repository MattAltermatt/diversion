Lichen — tide probe

Eden-type lattice growth on a zoned sea cliff. (Johnson–Mehl is the idea's source, not what this implements.)

How fast the decades run.

Average years between storms, with the wall-clock gap beside it — that figure moves when you change Years per minute. Each storm lands at an irregular interval around this, severity varies wildly on its own, and a storm takes several seconds to scrub through.

0 = the sea only ever does what the sea does. 100 = every storm carves something it has no business carving. The lichen grows back over it either way.

Exposed shores throw spray higher: wide, sharply drawn bands. Sheltered ones blur together.

New colonies landing on bare rock. Low = few big discs, high = a crowded mosaic.

How much the rock's own hollows and cracks hold water, bending the bands locally.

Darkening where two species meet. The reference photographs' most recognisable feature — map lichen is named for it — and what stops neighbouring greys reading as one mass. Drop it to 0 to see what it is doing.

Rock nothing can take hold on — quartz and smooth unweathered faces. Never heals, so it stays visible through everything and gives each rock a permanent signature.

0 = quartz veins following the rock's fractures. 100 = scattered smooth patches.

Species and zones after the documented supralittoral sequence — black Verrucaria at the fringe, yellow Caloplaca/Xanthoria in the splash, grey Ramalina/Lecanora in the dry zone. Boundaries freeze where two fronts meet; nobody overgrows anybody.
