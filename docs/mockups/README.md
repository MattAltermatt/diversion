| 2026-09-17 | [Kolam — sikku probe](2026-09-17-kolam.html) (interactive) | #349 the single-unbroken-line construction, built first and **superseded**. Dots are the VERTICES of a grid graph, the cells between them are its EDGES, and a spanning tree of those edges merges every dot's circle into exactly one closed curve — a kambi kolam. Verified in Chrome: 121 dots → 120 merges → **1 closed line**, every node degree 2, 484 arcs (= 121×4 exactly, since each join removes two dot-quarters and adds two connectors). The boundary needs no special case, which is the tell the model is right: an outer dot simply has fewer edges, so its unflipped outward quarters ARE the scalloped edge in the photographs. ⚠️ **What it cannot do is the reason it was superseded:** every part of the picture is the same quarter-arc at the same scale, so it produces one uniform loop-weave and can never yield concentric registers or motif variety. A *random* spanning tree also reads as a wandering maze — symmetric orbits, not one-edge-at-a-time merging, is what makes a single line look drawn rather than solved. Prior art: this generator family is already shipped as **Celtic** (#89), a 45° billiard on a checkerboard of crossings |
| 2026-09-17 | [Kolam — bundle-stroke probe](2026-09-17-kolam-bundle.html) (interactive) | #349 the question that actually decides the piece: **is the kolam look carried by the RENDERING rather than by a generator?** The composition here is deliberately Mandala's (#49) — concentric registers, N-fold motif repetition — because both candidate structures are already shipped, so what is under test is the drawing. Every path is stroked as a **bundle of parallel offset curves**, which nothing in this repo does (grepped: zero matches across Mandala and Celtic), and it is the dominant texture in every reference photograph. ⚠️ **The load-bearing parameter is the gap : width RATIO, not either alone** — at 1.2× the six strokes fuse into one fat white band and the effect vanishes entirely; at ~4× they read as a bundle.
